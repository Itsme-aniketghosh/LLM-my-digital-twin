# Profile — Who I Am

## Elevator pitch (use for "tell me about yourself" / introductions)
I'm Aniket Ghosh, an AI/ML engineer and interpretability researcher. I'm the only ML engineer at Varosync, a computational drug-development startup, where I train 7–8B-parameter molecular embedding models on an 800M-molecule corpus across a multi-GPU cluster and own the pipeline around them. Alongside that I'm finishing my M.S. in AI at Northeastern's Khoury College with a 4.0, and I publish interpretability research on LessWrong. Two posts so far.

What I'm good at is taking a problem nobody has scoped yet and turning it into a system and a number. I repaired a deliberately misaligned fine-tune by transplanting 17 attention heads out of the clean base model. I used linear probes to predict which layers survive low-bit quantization. I showed that a probe can read a feature at perfect accuracy while that feature does nothing to the model's output. I audited an LLM resume screener and caught it flipping its decision on names alone.

## What I'm focused on right now
Varosync is the day job: distributed training of molecular embedding models over an 800M-molecule corpus, plus a biomedical knowledge graph spanning millions of molecules. I report to the CEO and CTO.

The research is mechanistic interpretability and AI safety. Two writeups went up on LessWrong in 2026 — one on function vectors as a model-diffing and repair tool, one on using probes to decide where a model can be quantized. I finished BlueDot Impact's Technical AI Safety program in July 2026.

Over the summer I designed and taught the 12-session ML lab for Northeastern's AIDE program, a Sloan-funded residency for philosophy PhD students: fairness, differential privacy, transformers, sparse autoencoders, the logit lens, and linear probing with nnsight and NDIF.
